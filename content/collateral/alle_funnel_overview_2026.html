3 The 3rd meeting

Figures in this chapter are from the 13 Jul review. The tracker has no meeting-count field, so they were not recalculated.

27 of 101 prospects reached a 3rd meeting, so the pitch lands. The 3rd meeting is where deals are won or lost, and where we lose too many. The job is to convert the interest we already have: scope faster, send proposals faster, close better.

Meeting 1
Pitch, and the full price ladder (free POC, pilot, licence).
→
Meeting 2
Find the use case. Prospects often can't name one from our catalog.
→
Meeting 3
Scope it into a proposal, or let it go. Cover who owns it, ROI and integration.
Stopped at exactly 3 meetings: 10 of ~17 dropped without a proposal. They reached the 3rd meeting and we did not scope in time.
4 or more meetings: 7 of 10 reached a proposal. Staying in the deal is the best sign we have.
Warm leads get there more often. At the time, 45% of the internal team's leads reached a 3rd meeting, against 21% for Direct Sales and 17% for Greentern. Interest is not the problem. Scoping, proposals and closing are too slow: 15 of 27 (56%) heavily worked prospects were still lost (for example Versuni: CMO met 4 times, no proposal).

4 Takeaways

1
Funnel health is decent.
19% of leads got a proposal, 25% of proposals were won, and 27 leads reached a 3rd meeting.
2
Start with the CXO, especially business heads.
CIOs rarely have urgency. Their first thought is “can we build this in-house?” All five wins were business-led.
3
Don't ask for a use case. Give them two options to choose from.
About half of prospects can't name one.
4
Proposal to closing is the weak spot.
It may be price, or they may have no urgency and push it out. A quantified ROI is what lets our champion build the case internally.
5
The longer a proposal stays open, the less likely it closes.
11 are open now, and the oldest (Nippon, Schneider) went out in February. Push for a yes or a no.
